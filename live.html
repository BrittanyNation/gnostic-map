How to Live

Does the teaching say how to live, and what to eat? Not as a rulebook — there is no Gnostic Leviticus. What there is, is a direction, and it shows up everywhere once you see it: live in a way that feeds the spark and starves the sleep.

🌅 How to live

Wake people gently. The Gospel of Truth's whole ethic is in one picture: give rest to the weary, food to the hungry, and wake the sleepers — without contempt for the sleeping. You were asleep too.

Don't serve the house. No swearing loyalty to the powers of this world — not fate, not status, not the fear of the day. The Kingless Generation answers to no king down here. Practically, that meant refusing astrology as a master (the stars administer the prison; you may read the weather, you don't obey it), keeping your word without oaths, and holding possessions loosely.

Truthfulness and gentleness. The Pistis Sophia is almost severe here — its long lists of things to repent are mostly sins of the tongue and the temper: lying, slander, murmuring, rage. The awakened life is measured less by rituals than by whether you're becoming harmless and true.

Love as the fruit, not the fee. The Valentinians were blunt that the spark person isn't saved by good works — but a living spark shows: patience, generosity, no appetite for revenge. The works are the smoke, not the fire.

The strict wing — and it was the majority — added renunciation: simple living, little property, and for many, celibacy, because everything that ties you to the wheel was treated as a rope. The church writers accused a few small groups of the opposite extreme; that's mostly hostile reporting, and the surviving books don't back it up.

🕯️ How to repent while here

Repentance in these books is not a feeling. The word underneath it is metanoia — a turning: of the mind, of the direction, of the feet. Guilt alone changes nothing in the file; the counterfeit spirit loves guilt, because a soul can feel guilty for years and walk in the same circle the whole time. Turning is different. Turning moves.

How Sophia did it: the Pistis Sophia gives her thirteen repentances, and they are songs — sung upward, out loud, from inside the chaos, before she was lifted out of it. She did not wait to be rescued before she repented, and she did not repent once and stop. She kept turning, song after song, until the Light answered. That is the pattern the book holds up: repent from where you are, out loud, and keep at it.

Name it true. The Sophia's long repentance lists are mostly everyday things — lying, slander, murmuring, rage, cursing in temper. The file is written by the tongue and the temper more than by great crimes. So the first move is plain speech: say what you actually did, without the softer story. The file already has it true; your copy should match.

Undo what can be undone. Return what was taken. Repair what was broken. Apologize where an apology can still land, and repay where repaying is possible. The file records direction — and repair is direction you can see. What cannot be undone is carried to the Light instead, in the open, not buried.

Starve the pattern. Doing the same act again re-inks the line you just confessed. Part of repentance is changing the feed: the place, the hour, the company, the first small step that always leads to the same end. The awakened life in these books is practical like that — fences around the old road, not just sorrow about it.

And do it now. Repentance does not wait for death, and it is not priced by suffering. She was not raised because she suffered enough — she was raised because she turned. The file rewrites mid-life, in the middle of the assigned days, which means the cheapest place to change everything is exactly where you are standing.

🍞 What to eat

No single food code — but a strong lean: simple, plant-leaning, and light on wine. Many circles abstained from meat entirely (Pythagorean influence ran deep: eating flesh binds you to the flesh-world), and the Manichaean elect were strict vegetarians.

Wine gets the sharpest treatment, because drunkenness is the system's favorite picture of the human condition — the drunk soul that forgot itself. Moderation to abstinence was the norm in the strict schools.

And then Thomas drops the paradox that keeps it honest, saying 14: ritual fasting and diet rules, done as performance, accomplish nothing — "if you fast, you will bring sin upon yourselves." The fast that matters is saying 27: "If you do not fast from the world, you will not find the kingdom."

Which is why the Pearl story lands where it does: the prince falls asleep in Egypt right after he eats their food and wears their clothes. In this teaching, appetite is never just appetite. The real diet question is always: is this feeding the spark, or feeding the sleep?

So the summary the books would give: eat simply, stay sober, speak true, hold things loosely, owe the powers nothing — and spend your strength waking sleepers, starting with the ones at your own table.
